# H2O.ai db-benchmark — lazy pandas vs Polars

Faithful port of the [DuckDB Labs db-benchmark](https://github.com/duckdblabs/db-benchmark)
(group-by + join) against our lazy API and Polars. Generated by
`bench_h2o.py`; see `../docs/BENCHMARK_SUITES.md` for the suite background.

- **Scale:** 10,000,000 rows (1e7, ~0.5 GB), key cardinality k=100.
- **Hardware:** 8-core Apple Silicon laptop.
- **Timing:** `cold` = the first run (all caches cold — includes one-time
  process costs: acero thread-pool init and the per-column dictionary-encoding
  key-cache build). `hot` = min of 4 subsequent runs, i.e. steady state. Both
  engines are timed identically. Joins collect with `order="relaxed"` (H2O does
  not constrain output order — the fair comparison vs Polars, also unordered).
- **`hotx`** = Polars_hot / LazyPandas_hot. >1 means lazy pandas is faster.
- **Correctness:** all supported queries validated against Polars (group-by
  values within 1e-3; join row counts identical).

> **Methodology note (important).** An earlier version of this file timed only
> two runs ("cold, hot") with no warm-up. For the *first* Arrow-routed query in
> a process, *both* runs sat in the warm-up tail — so string-key q1 was
> reported at 114 ms when its steady state is ~14 ms, a pure measurement
> artifact. Steady-state numbers (min after warm-up) below are the meaningful
> comparison; the `cold` column preserves the real one-time first-query cost.

## Group-by (10M rows)

| query | LP cold (ms) | LP hot (ms) | PL hot (ms) | hotx |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q1: sum(v1) by id1 (str, 100 grp) | 262 | 14 | 18 | **1.23** |
| q2: sum(v1) by id1,id2 (str, 10k grp) | 138 | 21 | 140 | **6.59** |
| q3: sum(v1),mean(v3) by id3 (str, 100k grp) | 576 | 111 | 233 | **2.10** |
| q4: mean(v1,v2,v3) by id4 (int, 100 grp) | 22 | 20 | 13 | 0.65 |
| q5: sum(v1,v2,v3) by id6 (int, 100k grp) | 159 | 124 | 145 | **1.18** |
| q6: median(v3),std(v3) by id4,id5 | — | unsupported | 167 | — |
| q7: max(v1)-min(v2) by id3 | — | unsupported | 213 | — |
| q8: top2 v3 by id6 | — | unsupported | 208 | — |
| q9: corr(v1,v2)^2 by id2,id4 | — | unsupported | 271 | — |
| q10: sum(v3),count by id1..id6 (10M grp) | 1732 | 1913 | 1001 | 0.52 |

**Group-by: lazy pandas wins or ties 4 of the 6 supported queries.** String
keys (q1/q2/q3) are arrow-backed (`str` dtype in pandas 3.0) and route to
acero — they beat Polars at steady state (q2 by 6.6x). q4/q5 (numeric) hit the
acero path after the routing fix. The one genuine loss is **q10** (group by all
six columns → ~10M near-unique groups, 0.52x). Note the `cold` column: the
first Arrow-routed query pays a real one-time cost (q3 cold 576 ms vs hot
111 ms) for acero init + building the key dictionary cache.

## Join (10M rows)

| query | LP cold (ms) | LP hot (ms) | PL hot (ms) | hotx |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q1: inner join small on id1 | 441 | 402 | 231 | 0.58 |
| q2: inner join medium on id2 | 434 | 894 | 285 | 0.32 |
| q3: left join medium on id2 | 916 | 394 | 84 | 0.21 |
| q4: inner join medium on id5 (str key) | 543 | 873 | 134 | 0.15 |
| q5: inner join big on id3 (10M×10M) | 7245 | 5236 | 1026 | 0.20 |

**Joins are the real remaining gap (0.15–0.58x)** — and unlike the group-by
"gap", these survive warm-up, so they are genuine. Worst on the string-key
join (q4) and the 10M×10M build (q5), where string payload columns
(id4/id5) ride the gather and hit the Arrow `large_string` `take` wall.

## Findings

1. **Group-by is competitive-to-winning — the "string-key gap" was a
   benchmark artifact.** With warm-up excluded, q1/q2/q3 (string keys) beat
   Polars (1.2x–6.6x); the prior report's 0.19x/0.64x were measuring one-time
   acero init. No engine change was needed here — only the benchmark's timing.
2. **Numeric-key group-by routing — landed.** q4/q5 reach the acero path
   (`groupby_prefers_arrow`); q4 was 0.06x before that fix.
3. **Joins are the genuine gap (0.15–0.58x).** Worst: string-key join (q4
   0.15x) and 10M×10M inner (q5 0.20x). The next real target.
4. **q10** (group by 6 cols, ~10M groups) is the one slow group-by (0.52x).
5. **Four queries unsupported** — grouped `median` (q6), aggregation
   arithmetic (q7), grouped top-k + `explode` (q8), `corr` (q9).

### Priority order for closing the gap

1. ~~Numeric-key group-by~~ — **done** (q4 0.06x→0.65x, q5 →1.18x).
2. ~~String-key group-by~~ — **already faster** (benchmark artifact corrected).
3. **Joins** — string-key (q4 0.15x), large-build (q5 0.20x), left (q3 0.21x).
4. High-cardinality group-by q10 (~10M groups, 0.52x).
5. Grouped `median` + aggregation arithmetic (q6, q7) — unlocks 2 queries.
